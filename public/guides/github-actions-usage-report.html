How to read GitHub's Actions usage report

GitHub's billing page shows what Actions cost in total. The usage report shows where it went, down to the workflow file. It's the most accurate cost data you can get, because it's GitHub's own billing figures, not an estimate.

Summarized or detailed

  • The summarized report covers up to a year, grouped without user or workflow.
  • The detailed report covers up to 31 days and adds username and workflow_path. For per-workflow cost, it's the one you want.

The REST API's usage endpoint returns summarized data only. For workflow paths, download the detailed report.

Getting it

  1. Open the billing usage (metered usage) page for your account or organization.
  2. Click Get usage report at the top and pick the detailed report and date range.
  3. Click Email me the report. The download link arrives by email and expires after 24 hours. You can request one report at a time.

The columns

ColumnWhat it means
dateThe day the usage happened.
productThe product, such as Actions. Codespaces, Packages, and other products share the report.
skuThe runner or storage type, such as actions_linux or actions_macos. See runner pricing.
quantityHow much was used, in the row's unit_type.
unit_typeMinutes for compute rows. Storage rows use a storage unit, so their quantity is not minutes.
applied_cost_per_quantityThe rate applied to each unit.
gross_amountUsage at list price, before anything is discounted.
discount_amountThe part that isn't billed: included compute, free standard runners on public repositories, and self-hosted runners.
net_amountWhat's billable: gross_amount − discount_amount.
usernameWho triggered the usage. Detailed report only.
organizationThe organization the usage belongs to.
repositoryThe repository.
workflow_pathThe workflow file, such as .github/workflows/ci.yml. Detailed report only, and the column that makes per-workflow cost possible.
cost_center_nameThe cost center, if your enterprise uses them.

Gross, discount, and net

This is where most readings go wrong. gross_amount is what the usage would cost at list price. discount_amount takes out everything GitHub doesn't charge for. net_amount is what's left, and it's the number on the invoice.

That's why a busy public repository can show real gross dollars and a net of $0: standard runners there are free, and the whole gross amount shows up as discount. Included compute works the same way. The first $12, $18, or $300 a month, depending on plan, is discounted. Sort by gross and you see which workflow uses the most. Sort by net and you see what's actually billed.

Things that trip people up

  • Storage rows aren't minutes. Artifact and cache storage share the report with compute. Their quantity is in a storage unit, so adding the whole quantity column overstates minutes. Their dollars are real, though.
  • Many rows per workflow. A workflow appears once per day, per SKU, per user. Sum by repository plus workflow_path to get one figure per workflow.
  • Same file name, different repos. .github/workflows/ci.yml in twelve repositories is twelve workflows. Group by repository too.
  • Other products. Filter product to Actions if you only care about CI.

Skip the spreadsheet

spendtron does those sums in your browser. It groups by repository and workflow, ranks by net dollars (gross if everything nets to zero), names the top SKU for each workflow, and tells you whether the total is inside your plan's included compute. Nothing is uploaded.
